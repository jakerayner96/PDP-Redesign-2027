PDP redesign · GROUP UX · Nov 2026

Stakeholder
INTERVIEWS Report

We recently ran a series of UX discovery interviews with key stakeholders across Debenhams, boohoo, boohooMAN, PrettyLittleThing and Karen Millen about the PDP and its upcoming redesign. The aim was to get insights and an idea of scope / requirements from each brand before exploring any designs, ensuring that the PDP redesign delivers value across the Debenhams Group brands and fascias.

Jake Rayner, Paul Dossis, Demi Adesanya · Interviews conducted in Sep 2026

01

Summary

Most paid and search traffic lands on a PDP first. On boohooMAN, 46% of sessions reach a product page, but only 10% add to bag, and only 2 in 100 sessions check out. On Karen Millen, PDP abandonment is between 60 and 80%.

When trade is down, teams are asked to add more offers to the page, which costs margin. Stakeholders believe much of the lost conversion comes from how the product is presented on the page, not from price or offers.

  • Every stakeholder thinks the page is too busy. Ancillary services, multiple payment buttons, repeated delivery messages, accordions open by default and up to four recommendation components all dilute the important product information a user needs to make a purchase decision.
    A sideboard, a beauty product and a hoodie all need different information surfaced to the user. The fashion brands feel they inherited a department-store PDP during migration that has yet to be optimised for them.
  • Every fashion brand asked for matching sets in the new PDP. "Create/Shop The Look" and "Ways To Style" were mentioned several times. Where outfit building has previously been tested, it lifted AOV.
    It is still unclear to the brands how they would build these outfits (manually, or by system / AI) and how they would manage them on the new PDP.
  • Deliver+ (Seel) brings Debenhams a large share of its profit, but Karen Millen wants it at the bottom of the PDP, if not removed and only presented at checkout. boohooMAN says having it on the PDP puts German shoppers off.
  • The app is the biggest channel for fashion, and gets new features last. 50 to 60% of boohoo and PLT sales are on app, and Karen Millen's app converts best. boohooMAN's top ask is for Apple Pay express on the app PDP.
  • Many problems start in the product data before it reaches the page: dead colours, sizes nobody buys, unformatted marketplace copy and images at odd aspect ratios with gaps around them.
  • Functionality goes live without always being properly measured. Stakeholders reported that the sponsored carousel went live without a test and later turned out to be the worst performer, and that Subscribe & Save has no reporting. Every module on the new PDP needs tracking and a measurement plan.

03

Findings

01

The page is cluttered, and the clutter hides what sells

Every stakeholder raised this without being asked. Almost every request to the trading teams is to add something: a badge, a delivery method, membership, a promotion. Features go live one at a time, often without consultation, and when conversion is poor the usual response is to add more offers.

Every message has to go everywhere. And I think it just ends up being noise that drowns out the information that the customer is actually looking for.Debenhams
We just give way too many options on our PDP. Do you want Deliver+? Do you want Premier? PayPal or Apple Pay? Another product?Karen Millen
For consideration

Prioritise on the page everything a customer needs to decide to make a purchase decision, above every other component. The most important job the page has is to make this info easy for the user to find and interact with. Anything that isn't part of that decision (ancillary services, memberships, extra offers) becomes secondary or tertiary to this.

02

Different products need different pages

Home and furniture is Debenhams' biggest category, and non-fashion brings in more money than fashion for them. A furniture shopper wants to know the products dimensions, what its made of and when it will arrive. On today's PDP the product dimensions sit in an accordion and there is no surfaced delivery info.

The fashion brands feel they inherited a page built for a department store. Karen Millen wants a premium, editorial feel. boohooMAN wants it "super straightforward". boohoo and PLT asked whether fast fashion needs a PDP that works more like TikTok Shop. In practice that means a video-led page where you swipe between products, check your size and buy in one tap, with the detail tucked away.

As a customer… how big is it, what's it made of, what does it cost, how quickly can you deliver it to me? They're probably four very key things that you need to get across to me pretty damn quick.Debenhams
Customers are just used to swiping between products and just shopping TikTok Shop… they can see the video, they can see the product. They're not overly bothered about too much information as long as it's got the relevant size… do we need a PDP that is more like a social, TikTok style?boohoo · PLT
For consideration

Build a cohesive set of components and a differentiated layout per category (fashion, home and furniture, beauty), that still follows the group-wide ordering in finding 01, with a more intentional UI theme per brand set by our design system/design tokens. For fast fashion, explore a video-led, swipe-and-buy format along side a more streamlined PDP.

03

Bigger imagery, more video

Product images are king. Competitors PDP image carousels are much bigger, more modern and clearer than ours, on desktop particularly. Stakeholders asked for more product shot types (model, flat lay, fabric close-up) and video, which also helps paid social and shopping feeds. Marketplace images often arrive at odd ratios with white bands top and bottom. On app, the gallery stops at the last image instead of looping and images don't always fill their container.

For consideration

Explore bigger galleries: a desktop grid that takes at least half the page, full-width looping swipe on mobile web and app, more shot types and video, and a explore a solution for marketplace images in the incorrect aspect ratio (white bands top and bottom) if possible.

04

Premium has to put the product first and justify the price

Karen Millen’s top benchmarked sites were Reiss, ME+EM, Hobbs and Whistles. These sites all have a much more premium look and feel to them. Beyond the key purchase info identified in section 01, KM have requested more editorial content on the PDP, prioritised over outfits, recommendations and ancillary services.

Today the page looks the same in a heavy sale and a full-price campaign, and full-price products need more editorial content on the PDP that explain the elevated price - "this is why this coat is £250".

For consideration

Give premium brands their own theme inside the group framework: an image-led hero, then description and size before any services, a distinct full-price look with detail editorial and storytelling components, differentiated from reduced price and sale formats.

05

Size, fit and colour problems start in the product data

Discontinued colours stay on the site, crossed out. Dropdowns list sizes that were never bought, and legacy codes ("30" vs "30R") sit side by side. Some PDPs use size boxes, others dropdowns. boohooMAN buys hoodies in muscle, boxy and slim fits and in Plus and Tall, but the page doesn't show it. Trousers should ask for leg length, then waist. Beauty shades appear in a random order. A more granular and optimised size selection was one of boohooMAN’s top asks.

For consideration

Clean up product data at source: remove dead colours and unsold sizes, standardise size codes, and add all fit and leg-length attributes, with the ability for the user to easily select. Align size selection from one consistent set of components and only nuance where an uplift in conversion is identified.

06

Too much or too little - delivery, returns, promotions, payments

Delivery and returns appear in "about 20 different places" across the journey (USP bar, badges, accordions, delivery protection), yet a furniture PDP has no delivery date displayed. Promo codes appear in the USP bar, are badged throughout the journey and again on the PDP - is this too much? An earlier test showed a closed product description beat an open one, but it has since been reopened. Express checkout buttons stack up and create choice overwhelm at a critical conversion point. Klarna is 70 to 80% of Netherlands sales but is not surfaced on the PDP.

For consideration

Use one delivery module with a "get it by" date, clearly state returns policy in one clear default place, introduce one promo slot with multiple offers behind this, clean and dedupe product data, re-test accordions closed by default, and explore fewer express payment buttons if possible, within what the contracts allow.

Ensure that everything on the PDP fits into the prioritised order outlined in point 01 and has dedicated slots, each with clear purpose, clearly comunicating the right balance of product, payment, promotions, delivery and returns info along side additional reccomendations and ancillary services where appropriate

07

Ancillary services make money and take up a lot of the page

Deliver+ (Seel), Unlimited / Premier, XCover, Task Rabbit and Subscribe & Save take up to a third of a Debenhams PDP. Seel is attached to about 25% of Debenhams orders and is "a significant proportion of our overall profit". The fashion brands see it differently. Karen Millen says it is irrelevant on the PDP and would move it to the bottom of the page or off the page entirely. boohooMAN says it puts German shoppers off.

It's worth a lot of money. Don't mess with it. Don't do anything without testing it.Debenhams
That should be right at the bottom or perhaps even at checkout… it's irrelevant at PDP.Karen Millen
For consideration

Test placements: remove vs a compact row vs lower down on the page, bag or checkout only, and different rules by category, brand and region. Ensure we hold the attach rate, on Debenhams specifically.

The commercial owners of Deliver+ need to sign off any test. Non-debenhams stakeholders are not sure of the contractual or commercial obligations with Seel and what options we have to explore. Further business direction needed prior to any exploration.

08

Outfits and sets are the biggest growth idea

The fashion brands described three separate needs, often all called "bundles":

  • Matching set: the hoodie, sweat and jogger, or bikini top and bottom. boohooMAN will sell all sets as separates from Q1.
  • Shop the look: the exact items the model is wearing in the product image. The studio/ecom teams want to supply ensure the right SKUs are being listed, because an AI match can get them wrong (e.g. not the matching denim wash).
  • Ways to style: several looks for different occasions (e.g. M&S style flat lays, ASOS dress up / dress down).

Home and beauty also need similar patterns, with a nuanced use case: a bed with a duvet and pillows, a skincare bundle & routine.

If you look at a picture… he's got a really nice t-shirt underneath, he's got a pair of jeans, and you actually go, I just want to look like that guy.boohooMAN
For consideration

Review our existing components (carousels, grids, bundles) and bolster with new ones for matching sets, shop the look and ways to style, each with its own data source and managed by the brands. We then decide whether one flexible component covers every use case across the group, or each use case gets its own.

09

Recommendations: fewer zones, each with one job

PDPs can carry up to four product carousels plus an SEO block. The same product sometimes appears more than once in a single carousel, and some recommendations lead customers away into categories that have nothing to do with what they're looking at. On Debenhams, the sponsored products carousel went live without a test, and when it was tested later the original version was the worst performer. On boohoo, "Handpicked for you" has worked well. boohoo and PLT asked whether we need four zones at all vs one continuous reccs component that is more personalised/optimised for the individual shopper.

Each brand also wants something different from recommendations. boohooMAN wants more of the same look, not alternatives. Debenhams wants inspiration and products that go with the item. boohoo and PLT want a mix, but put more emphasis on which specific products were shown - their own-brand products first, then other group brands, then marketplace, with plenty more to browse after that - rather than the format of the recommendations.

For consideration

Test different configurations and orders of PDP zones down the page by job rather than by our existing component format - things that go well with this product (complete the look, matching sets), then things to buy in addition to it or instead of it. This can be product or services. Then design and implement the components to support that. Each zone label should clearly describe what it does, if required at all. Products to be deduped, atomic tests on component style (carousel or grid, number of rows) and the tech/logic powering it independently. Every zone continually tracked and optimised.

Further explorations into personalisation to make reccomendations hyper relevant to the user should also be considered, espcially for PLT and Boohoo.

10

Who sees what should depend on the customer

boohoo and PLT want recommendations based on what the customer is looking at, what they've looked at before and what we think they'll want to buy. Premier and Royalty members currently see messages that don't apply to them: "free returns in 21 days" or "next day delivery from £5.99" isnt as relevant to a Premier/Royalty customer, because they already get free next-day delivery. Debenhams wants to stop repeating messages the customer has already seen, such as a code from the banner they clicked, and to stop showing home and garden offers to someone who only buys beauty. boohooMAN wants to change what is prominent by region.

For consideration

Decide for each module what changes with who is looking: member status, browsing and purchase history, category and region. Members see their benefits, not the upsell. Personalisation decides what fills each zone and what is populated on page on a per-user-type and per-region basis.

11

Social proof should come from what customers do

Debenhams wants reviews moved up from the bottom of the page and made richer: fit and quality for fashion, skin type for beauty. boohooMAN says German shoppers want ratings, reviews and secure payments before they trust the product or brand. boohoo and PLT have no reviews: the contract was renewed without boohoo, however products don't live long enough to collect them, and boohoo reviews from Debenhams customers averaged 2.8 to 3.2. They would rather show wishlist counts from our own data.

For consideration

Optimise reviews UI, make reviews a movable module each brand can switch on and place, with fit and quality information surfaced where we have it. Test wishlist counts as social proof on boohoo and PLT. Reconsider taggstar UI, placement and frequency, and reposition as secondary to the above for social proof.

12

App first in revenue, last in features

50 to 60% of boohoo and PLT sales are on app. Karen Millen is about 80% mobile and its app converts best. The app still gets changes months after web. The brands are aware there are resourcing and tehcnical challenges that lead to this, but the ask remains. Apple Pay express on the app PDP was the single most urgent ask from boohooMAN.

For consideration

Design web and app together from the same components (some native nuance will still exist), ship and measure on web, then move to app.

13

We can't see what works

The brands reported that there is no robust per-widget reporting and no PDP funnel, the move from PowerBI to Looker has left gaps, and the heatmap and session-replay tool the business used to have (Contentsquare) has gone. When trade is down, teams are left with no levers other than to run promotions and give away margin.

For consideration

Ship every module with tracking and a test plan, keep a test register, and bring back heatmaps and session replay (Contentsquare or an alternative).

What this means for the next tests

  • In Home and Beauty, lots of modules moved at once, so it was hard to understand what caused the drop.
  • Future tests need to be more atomic: change one thing at a time and measure its impact.
  • That is harder when the PDP format and UI are changing at the same time, so the test plan for the redesign needs to allow for it.
07

What happens next

Principles for the new PDP

  • Prioritise the info that helps someone decide to purchase this product over anything else on the page.
  • Every module has a job, an owner and a metric. We continuously measure at a granular level of detail (each click/tap, scroll etc.)
  • One framework, one design system: layouts per category and UI themes per brand, with no forked pages.
  • Delivery, returns, promotions and payments each appear clearly, once, in a purpose designed position, not repeated.
  • Test anything that touches profit and deliver+ uptake, check attach rates in every test.
  • Where the problem is in the data, fix the data. This is a key enabler prior to the new PDP going live, the front end can only do so much.
  • Ship on web first, then fast follow on app once value is proven, if not start to test on app. Ensure the same components are used app as are used on web (unless native nuance justifies), enabling operational efficiency and pace of delivery, as well as a consistent experience for users.

Concept phase

UX to explore multiple designs, prototypes and concepts for every fascia, on mobile, desktop and app, built on the group design system, before reviewing and feeding back with the team.

IDEATION & BENCHMARKING WORKSHOPS

UX will run a series of ideation and benchmarking workshops, starting with the UX, Tech and Product team, then expanding to brands if required. In these sessions, we will share examples of what good looks like from other competitors, discuss what might be worth exploring for us and review any designs and concepts created by UX.

A/B & CRO TEST PLAN & OPTIMISATION

UX will work hand in hand with Experimentation team to break down concepts and designs we wish to progress into a robust testing plan, and ensure experimentation team’s involvement in the project from the outset.
