01

Summary

Most paid and search traffic lands on a PDP first (43% of boohooMAN web sessions start on one), and most shoppers leave from there. On boohooMAN, 46% of sessions reach a product and 10% add to bag. On Karen Millen, 60 to 80% of PDP visits are abandoned. When trade is down, the teams add more offers to the page, which costs margin and leaves the page as it was.

  • Everyone thinks the page is too busy, and nobody would keep it as it is. Ancillary services, payment buttons, repeated delivery messages, open accordions and up to four recommendation carousels push the product out of view.
  • A sideboard, a beauty routine and a hoodie need different information at the top of the page. The fashion brands also feel they inherited a department-store page.
  • Every fashion brand asked for matching sets, "shop the look" and "ways to style". Where outfit building has been tested, it lifted AOV. Today the outfit data is put together by hand.
  • Deliver+ (Seel) brings Debenhams a large share of its profit, and the fashion brands want it off the PDP. No contract says where it has to go, so we'll test placements and hold the attach rate.
  • The app is the biggest channel and gets new features last. 50 to 60% of boohoo and PLT sales are on app, and Karen Millen's app converts best. boohooMAN's top ask is Apple Pay express on the app PDP.
  • Many of the problems start in the catalogue: dead colours, sizes nobody bought, unformatted marketplace copy, images at odd ratios and bundles made by hand.
  • Modules go live without being measured. The sponsored carousel went on without a test and later turned out to be the worst performer, and Subscribe & Save has no reporting. Each module on the next PDP needs a measurement plan.

03

Findings

01

The page is cluttered, and the clutter hides what sells

Every stakeholder raised this without being asked. Almost every request to the trading teams is to add something: a badge, a delivery method, membership, a promotion. Features go live one at a time and nobody looks at the page as a whole. Debenhams heatmaps show many elements that hardly anyone touches.

Every message has to go everywhere. And I think it just ends up being noise that drowns out the information that the customer is actually looking for.Debenhams ecommerce
We're just given way too many options on our PDP. Do you want Deliver+? Do you want Premier? PayPal or Apple Pay? Another product?Karen Millen
So we willDesign a "decision zone". Above Add to bag, show only what helps someone choose this product. Everything else goes below, and each module there has an owner and a metric.
02

Different products need different pages

Home and furniture is Debenhams' biggest category, and non-fashion brings in more money than fashion. A furniture shopper wants four answers fast: how big, what's it made of, what does it cost, when can it arrive. On today's sideboard PDP the dimensions sit in an accordion and there is no delivery date. The fashion brands feel they inherited a page built for a department store. Karen Millen wants a premium, editorial feel. boohooMAN wants it "super straightforward". A category A/B test (fashion, home, beauty) is already being set up with Becca.

As a customer… how big is it, what's it made of, what does it cost, how quickly can you deliver it to me? They're probably four very key things that you need to get across to me pretty damn quick.Debenhams leadership
So we willBuild one set of components with a layout per category (fashion, home and furniture, beauty), a theme per brand set by design tokens, and a key facts block for each category. Start with the categories that make the most money.
03

Outfits and sets are the biggest growth idea

The fashion brands described three separate needs, often all called "bundles":

  • Matching set: the hoodie, sweat and jogger, or bikini top and bottom, switchable at the top of the page (as Reiss does). boohooMAN will sell all sets as separates from Q1.
  • Shop the look: the exact items the model is wearing. The studio has to supply the right SKU and wash, because an AI match gets them wrong.
  • Ways to style: several looks for different occasions (Karen Millen's "7-day dressing", M&S flat lays, ASOS dress up / dress down).

Home and beauty need the same patterns: a bed with a duvet and pillows, a skincare routine. A Karen Millen outfit test lifted AOV "massively", but outfits are built by hand across thousands of styles.

If you look at a picture… he's got a really nice t-shirt underneath, he's got a pair of jeans, and you actually go, I just want to look like that guy.boohooMAN
So we willDesign three distinct components (Matching set, Shop the look, Ways to style), each with its own data source, and work with Product and PIM on feeding outfit data from the studio at shoot time.
04

Ancillary services make money and take up a lot of the page

Deliver+ (Seel), Unlimited / Premier, XCover, Task Rabbit and Subscribe & Save take up to a third of a Debenhams PDP. Seel is attached to about 25% of Debenhams orders and is "a significant proportion of our overall profit". The fashion brands see it differently. Karen Millen calls it irrelevant on a premium PDP and would move it to checkout, and boohooMAN says it puts German shoppers off. No contract sets its size or placement.

It's worth a lot of money. Don't mess with it. Don't do anything without testing it.Debenhams leadership
That should be right at the bottom or perhaps even at checkout… it's irrelevant at PDP.Karen Millen
So we willTest placements: a compact row below the CTAs, bag or checkout only, and different rules by category, brand and region. Every variant has to hold the attach rate. Members stop seeing the membership upsell.
05

Recommendations: fewer zones, each with one job

Pages carry up to four carousels plus an SEO block. Labels like "We think you'll like" and "Handpicked for you" mean nothing to shoppers. The same product appears several times in one carousel, and some modules send people back to other categories. The Debenhams sponsored carousel went on without a test and turned out to be the worst-performing variant. "Handpicked for you" has worked well on boohoo. The brands want different mixes: boohooMAN wants more of the same look rather than alternatives, Debenhams wants inspiration and complements, and boohoo and PLT want own brand first with a long browse below.

So we willUse at most three zones below the CTAs: complete it, alternatives, recently viewed. Each label says what the zone does, products are deduped by style with colour swatches, each brand picks the display (carousel or grid) and the engine behind it, and every widget is reported on.
06

Size, fit and colour problems start in the product data

Discontinued colours stay on the page, crossed out. Dropdowns list sizes that were never bought, and legacy codes ("30" vs "30R") sit side by side. Some PDPs use size boxes, others dropdowns. boohooMAN buys hoodies in muscle, boxy and slim fits and in Plus and Tall, but the page doesn't show it. Trousers should ask for leg length, then waist. Beauty shades appear in a random order. Size is the boohooMAN CEO's top topic.

So we willDesign one variant selector (range → fit → colour → size), with display rules that hide dead colours and never-bought sizes, and agree a data clean-up with Product.
07

Say it once: delivery, returns, promotions, payments

Delivery and returns appear in "about 20 different places" (USP bar, badges, accordions, delivery protection), yet a furniture PDP has no delivery date. Promo codes appear in the USP bar, on the badge and again on the page. An earlier test showed a closed product description beat an open one, but it has since been reopened. Express checkout buttons stack up, and only PayPal is contractual (as stated; to be confirmed).

So we willUse one delivery and returns module with a "get it by" date, one promo slot with dedupe rules, accordions closed by default, and one device-native express button alongside the contracted PayPal.
08

Bigger imagery, more video

Competitors give images at least half the desktop page and the full width on mobile. Stakeholders asked for more shot types (model, flat lay, fabric close-up) and video, which also helps paid social and shopping feeds. Marketplace images arrive at odd ratios with white bands. On app, the gallery stops at the last image instead of looping.

So we willBuild a desktop image grid with a sticky buy column, test full bleed against the current peek on mobile, add video to the gallery, make it loop, and normalise marketplace images to 2:3.
09

App first in revenue, last in features

50 to 60% of boohoo and PLT sales are on app. Karen Millen is about 80% mobile and its app converts best. The app still gets changes months after web. Apple Pay express on the app PDP was the single most urgent ask from boohooMAN.

So we willDesign web and app together from the same components, ship and measure on web, then move to app on the new glass wrapper.
10

We can't see what works

There is no per-widget reporting and no PDP funnel, the move from PowerBI to Looker has left gaps, and the heatmap and session-replay tool we used to have (Contentsquare) has gone. When trade is down, teams run promotions and give away margin.

So we willShip every module with tracking and a test plan, keep a test register, and ask for Becca's existing heatmap and interaction data before removing anything.

What has already been tested

  • Product description closed beat open (Debenhams). It was rolled out and has since been reopened.
  • The original four-carousel set-up, including sponsored products, was the worst performer. A new variant now converts better (Debenhams).
  • Outfit building lifted AOV "massively" (Karen Millen, with Becca).
  • "Handpicked for you" performs strongly (boohoo, PLT).
  • Continuous gallery vs pagination: no negative impact on conversion (pre-migration).
08

What happens next

Principles for the new PDP

  • Above the CTAs, show only what helps someone choose this product.
  • Every module has a job, an owner and a metric.
  • One system: layouts per category and themes per brand, with no forked pages.
  • Delivery, returns, promotions and payments each appear once.
  • Test anything that touches profit, and check attach rates in every test.
  • Where the problem is in the data, fix the data.
  • Ship on web first, then take the same components to app.

Concept phase

Working prototypes for every fascia, on mobile, desktop and app, built on the group design system so people can click through each idea and compare them:

  • The decision zone, with fashion, home and furniture, and beauty layouts.
  • Matching set, Shop the look and Ways to style.
  • One delivery and returns module with a date.
  • Ancillary placement variants, ready for test.
  • The variant selector (range, fit, colour, size).
  • A configurator, so trading teams can reorder and toggle modules and preview real SKUs.

After that we play the concepts back to each brand and build a test roadmap with Becca.
